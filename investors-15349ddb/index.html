On the road

One 26 ft box truck, 26,000 lb GVWR or less. Everything rides inside.

Roof slides out

Rear deck built

The rear door drops level and ten 4 × 8 platforms on six legs each complete a 15 ft L.

Built for a crowd

Checked to a 100 psf crowd load

The deck is designed for the building-code load on assembly platforms, 100 lb per square foot, with railings that hold a 200 lb push at the top. The design uses tight joist spacing, six legs under each platform, support legs at the truck's hinge line and rear sill, mid-length legs under the rear door, and ground pads under every leg. A structural engineer stamps the final drawings.

Wedding layout

Everything rides in the one truck, with no support vehicle: deck, roof, rails, stairs, bar, seating, tables, ice, drinks, water and crew. Carbon fiber roof beams, rafters and legs, carbon fiber sail and rail posts, honeycomb stair treads and lighter interior finishes keep the fully loaded truck about 600 lb under a typical 10,000 lb payload (about 430 lb with the lounge set). Engineering progress

Hinge, stairs and platform legs

Shop-ready: the deck hinge, the folding stairs for the second exit, and fold-flat platform legs that level on uneven ground.

Stair close-up, 18 s: the guard panels lift off, the treads fold flat into the stringers, and it sets up again in reverse.

Deck hinge

Folds flat

The hinge pin sits 1-1/4 in below the leaf undersides on 2 in knuckles, so the leaves fold flat. Leg stands carry the crowd load, not the pins. Rated 100 psf, with a 12 in stowage pocket.

Folding stairs

Shop drawings and cut list

Two 44 in stairs, 7 risers of 6-7/8 in, 1/4 in aluminum plate stringers. Each one hooks onto the deck and stands on two leveling feet, folds to about 10 in thick and weighs about 180 lb.

Build files

CAD

Real-size CAD for the hinge and stair in FreeCAD (STEP export) and OpenSCAD (flat DXF for a cutting shop).

Platform legs: each of the 60 legs folds flat under its platform, telescopes in 1/2 in steps and finishes on a 5,000 lb swivel leveling foot for uneven ground. Folding knee braces and clip-on cross braces stop the deck from swaying under a crowd, and the platforms bolt to each other every 24 in. The outer side-deck leaf is honeycomb panel and the lounge set is aluminum outdoor furniture. Startup is $200,377 for the Light build, or $193,646 for the Western build with cedar floors.

The numbers

Annual scenarios

Three one-year scenarios for Oregon: 15 event days a year on the low end, 30 on the high end, and 22 in between, at a $6,000 base rate per event day with the host supplying the alcohol. Alcohol supplied by the bar, extra hours and other add-ons are billed separately and not counted. Break-even after owner pay is about 10 to 17 event days a year. Demand at $6,000 a day is unproven; it is about twice what trailer bars charge, so the day-rate options below run the same model at $5,000, $4,000 and $3,000.

Licensing and permits

Private events, public sales, game days

Licensing depends on whether guests pay for drinks. Oregon first; other states through a local licensed partner.

Private events

No OLCC license

Weddings, corporate parties and private tailgates where the host buys the alcohol and guests drink free. The $6,000 day rate covers the truck, deck, bar and staff; servers are still trained. Alcohol supplied by the bar is an add-on.

Public sales

OLCC caterer or event license

Festivals, cash bars and ticketed watch parties. Requires $300k liquor liability, food at every event and OLCC server permits. About 30 to 90 days to license.

Ducks game days

Private land, not the lots

Autzen lots ban alcohol sales and oversized rigs. Game-day work is private hosted tailgates or licensed watch parties on private property nearby.

Investor money is raised privately under SEC Rule 506(b) with an attorney. Planning guide, not legal advice; details and sources are in the Legal and Permits Guide.

Pros and cons

Pros, cons and risks

Pros

  • A venue, not a counter. 670 sq ft of railed, partly covered deck in one booking.
  • No CDL. The largest truck a regular license allows, so drivers are easy to staff.
  • Built for Oregon weather. The sliding roof keeps rain off the side deck.
  • Built in-house. An experienced metal and carpentry crew keeps the build to materials.
  • Built for a crowd. Checked to a 100 psf crowd load; up to 136 people standing with two stairs.
  • Silent power. A 15 kWh lithium pack under the frame runs two events per charge, no generator noise at weddings.
  • One trip. Platforms, rails, sails and stair all pack inside the box.
  • Many revenue streams. Weddings, corporate, festivals, wineries and deck-only rentals.

Cons and risks

  • Not built yet. Requires engineer sign-off and a first build.
  • Seasonality. Peak May to October; holiday corporate events fill some of winter.
  • Licensing work. Public sales need food at every event and $300k liquor coverage.
  • Weight. Fully loaded, the truck runs about 600 lb under a typical 10,000 lb payload, and that depends on the carbon fiber and other lighter parts. The truck must be chosen for payload and weighed before purchase.
  • Setup and wind. A small crew and a few hours; roof and sails retract above 20 mph.
  • Price untested. $6,000 a day is about twice what trailer bars charge. The product is the venue, not the drinks; demand is unproven. At $4,000 it still pays back in about four years at 22 days; at $3,000 it does not cover owner pay.

The founder

Max Edson

Eugene native and working bartender, from a legendary Maui saloon to Eugene's B2 Bar and Grill.

Max was born and raised in Eugene, Oregon, and graduated from South Eugene High School. Soon after graduation he moved to Maui, where he tended bar at Charley's Restaurant & Saloon in Paia, the island saloon long known for Willie Nelson and the musicians who played there.

He then worked in the state-licensed cannabis industry, first in Northern California and then back in Eugene. Returning to hospitality, he joined Hot Mama's Wings, where he learned more about running a restaurant, and today he tends bar at B2 Bar and Grill, serving a loyal crowd of longtime Eugene regulars.

The Bar Truck is Max's idea: the warmth of a classic saloon, brought to weddings and events across Oregon. Max lives in Eugene with his son, Lucas.
